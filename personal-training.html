My Transformation

Transformation in Progress

Starting Point

I began this transformation in August 2024 at a bodyweight of 80 kg and an estimated body-fat level of approximately 22%.

My main goal was to build enough muscle to create a strong physique, then cut down until my six-pack became clearly visible.

Current Progress

I currently weigh 68 kg, with an estimated body-fat level of approximately 16%.

I have made significant progress, but I do not consider the transformation finished. I am continuing to reduce body fat while maintaining as much muscle and strength as possible.

Goal

Build a muscular, athletic physique and become lean enough to clearly see my six-pack.

My goal was not simply to lose weight. I wanted to build a solid base of muscle first, then gradually cut down to reveal the physique underneath.

Approach

My approach was to spend time building muscle across my entire body, then move into a controlled calorie deficit to reduce body fat while continuing to train hard.

The process has focused on consistency rather than shortcuts: structured training, accurate calorie tracking, high effort and patience over time.

Training Split

I use a high-frequency hybrid split that combines elements of full-body, upper/lower and push-pull training. It is not a traditional bro split or a pure full-body split.

MondayChest, back and triceps
TuesdayLegs, shoulders and biceps
ThursdayBack, chest, shoulders and abs
FridayLegs, biceps and forearms
SaturdayChest, back, triceps and abs

The split is designed so that each session emphasises different muscle groups while allowing the muscles trained hardest the previous day to recover. This creates frequent exposure to each major muscle group without repeating the exact same session structure.

Training Philosophy

I believe training needs to be challenging enough to create a reason for the body to adapt. Most working sets are taken very close to failure, with a strong focus on controlled technique, progressive overload and honest effort.

Progress is not always visible from one session to the next. A single rep or small strength increase may seem insignificant, but those improvements compound over months and years.

Strength Progression

Strength progression was measured through improved exercise performance, including more repetitions, better control, increased resistance and stronger execution across key movements.

Over time, I became noticeably stronger in movements such as weighted pull-ups, dips, pressing exercises, leg training and isolation work.

Nutrition

During the cutting phase, I used an intake of approximately 1,800 calories per day while prioritising protein, accurate food tracking and meals that supported training performance.

The goal was to create a consistent calorie deficit without relying on extreme fasting or random restriction. Food quality matters, but calorie intake, protein and consistency are the main controllable factors.

Progress

The transformation remains in progress. I have moved from 80 kg to 68 kg, while my estimated body-fat level has moved from approximately 22% to approximately 16%.

These body-fat figures are estimates rather than laboratory-tested measurements. I am currently continuing the cutting phase while working to maintain muscle, strength and training performance.

Lessons Learned

The biggest lesson has been that unnecessary dirty bulking usually creates more fat to lose later without guaranteeing better muscle gain.

A controlled approach works better: understand calorie intake, track food accurately, train consistently and minimise unplanned overeating. The aim is to stay consistent across the week and return to the plan after imperfect days.

Another major lesson is that physique development takes time. The work from a single day may not look important, but the accumulated effect of hundreds of sessions becomes visible over one or two years.

Personal Reflection

I have wanted a visible six-pack and a lean, muscular physique for a long time. I am not fully at the end goal yet, but I am much closer than when I began.

This transformation has taught me that real progress comes from repeating the basics for long enough: training hard, tracking honestly, managing nutrition and continuing even when the visual changes feel slow.

I am proud of the progress so far, but I also know there is more to improve. That is what keeps me motivated. I do not see fitness as a temporary challenge. I see it as a long-term process of becoming stronger, leaner and more capable.

Personalised Recipe Reinvention

Keep Eating the Meals You Love.

Together, we will reinvent your favourite meals so they better support your fitness goals without turning them into something completely different.

Included with coaching

Personalised recipe reinvention is included as a standard part of every personal training and coaching program.

I do not believe you need to give up your favourite foods to make progress.

As part of your coaching program, we will sit down together and go through the meals you genuinely enjoy. Then we will rebuild those recipes around your calorie, protein and nutrition targets while keeping each meal as close as possible to the original.

If you love a Big Mac, the goal is not to turn it into a Big Mac bowl. The goal is to help you make an actual Big Mac with the bun, patties, cheese, sauce, pickles and full experience, then make thoughtful changes that better suit your goals.

The same approach applies to your favourite pasta, curry, pizza, burger, dessert or family recipe. This is about making the food you already love work for you.

Your Favourite Meal → Rebuilt for Your Goals

A practical process that keeps the identity of the original meal while helping it fit your wider nutrition plan.

Keep the Meal. Change the Strategy.

Substitutions can be useful when they reflect what a client actually enjoys. They should not be forced replacements that remove the experience they wanted.

Generic Diet Replacement
  • “You like burgers? Eat a burger bowl.”
  • “You like pizza? Use a plain wrap.”
  • “You like dessert? Replace it with yoghurt.”
My Approach
  • “You like burgers? Let’s build you a better burger.”
  • “You like pizza? Let’s create a pizza that fits your goals.”
  • “You have a favourite dessert? Let’s reinvent the actual dessert.”
What Is Included

A recipe you can confidently make at home.

As part of your personal training program, I will work with you to:

  • Learn your favourite meals, foods and flavours.
  • Understand which parts of each meal matter most to you.
  • Recreate the complete meal rather than an unrelated alternative.
  • Adjust ingredients, quantities and cooking methods where appropriate.
  • Calculate estimated calories and macronutrients.
  • Personalise the serving size around your goals.
  • Create a clear recipe you can make at home.
  • Provide practical ingredient substitutions where useful.
  • Help the meal fit your overall nutrition plan.
  • Refine the recipe after you try it and share feedback.
Core Philosophy

Nutrition should still feel like your life.

Rigid plans are difficult to sustain when they completely change the way you enjoy food. Instead of handing every client the same meal plan, I help you build a personalised collection of meals you genuinely look forward to eating.

The goal is not to make every meal as low in calories as physically possible. It is to find the best balance for your needs.

Taste Satisfaction Protein Calories Convenience Affordability Long-term consistency

The finished recipe should still feel like your favourite meal, not a disappointing diet substitute.

Preserve What Makes It Worth Eating.

The ingredients may change thoughtfully, but the flavour, texture and experience that made you love the meal should remain.

Big Mac

A complete homemade burger.

Buns, patties, cheese, pickles, onions, lettuce and Big Mac-style sauce. Adjustments might include leaner meat, carefully measured sauce, suitable cheese and better portion control.

Carbonara

Creamy pasta that feels genuine.

Preserve the flavour and texture you enjoy, with thoughtful changes to pasta quantity, protein source, sauce ingredients and serving size.

Pizza

A real pizza with a proper base.

Keep the base, sauce, cheese and toppings, then redesign the quantities to support your calorie and protein targets while still feeling like pizza.

Family and cultural recipes are welcome.

Bring the recipes you grew up eating or the meals your family regularly makes. Food can carry emotional, cultural and social meaning. The goal is to improve how a recipe supports your goals without stripping away what makes it meaningful.

Why This Matters

Skills and meals you can keep.

  • Continue enjoying your favourite foods.
  • Feel less restricted around food.
  • Reduce the cycle of avoiding and craving favourite meals.
  • Build practical cooking skills.
  • Understand portions and ingredients.

Practical Nutrition Coaching

Personalised education, not a rigid prescription.

This is fitness nutrition coaching and practical recipe development within my professional scope. It is not a chef service, medical nutrition treatment or a claim that one recipe suits everyone.

Your targets, preferences, budget, cooking confidence and feedback guide the process. Where specialised dietary support is needed, I will recommend working with an appropriately qualified professional.
